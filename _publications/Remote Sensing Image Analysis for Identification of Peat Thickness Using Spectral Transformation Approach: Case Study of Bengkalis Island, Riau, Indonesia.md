## Abstract
Peatlands play an important role in the global climate. Balancing economic, social, and conservation needs in peatland utilization is essential for sustainable management. Peat depth is a key parameter for determining appropriate land use. However, rapid peat depth mapping remains challenging due to dynamic land-use changes across large peatland areas. This study uses multi-temporal remote sensing data to analyze peatland-related land-use changes. Vegetation and wetness indices derived from spectral transformations were evaluated. Correlation and regression analysis were applied for modeling, and accuracy was assessed using the Standard Error of Estimate (SEE). The results indicate that vegetation indices and NDSI were not reliable for peat thickness modeling due to unstable vegetation and land cover changes. In contrast, NDWI showed better performance and achieved a peat thickness estimation accuracy of 41.96%. Future research should consider improved sampling design, better distribution of samples in preserved land covers, and inclusion of additional environmental variables. Vegetation and wetness indices still hold potential as alternative variables for peat depth mapping.
